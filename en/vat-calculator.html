Frequently Asked Questions
What is VAT and how does it work?+
VAT (Value Added Tax) is an indirect tax on the consumption of goods and services. The end consumer bears the tax, which is included in the selling price. Businesses collect it on behalf of HMRC and pay it periodically. In the UK, VAT was introduced in 1973 and is governed by the Value Added Tax Act 1994.
How do you extract VAT from a price?+
To extract VAT from a VAT-inclusive price, use: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + rate). Example at 20%: £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100 net + £20 VAT. Useful for businesses that need to separate the VAT element from a total price for accounting purposes.
What are the UK VAT rates in 2026?+
The UK has three VAT rates: 20% standard rate (most goods and services), 5% reduced rate (domestic energy, children's car seats, some home energy-saving materials), 0% zero rate (most food, children's clothing, books, newspapers, public transport, new residential buildings).
Who is exempt from VAT?+
Certain supplies are VAT exempt (no VAT charged, no input tax recovery): medical services, education, financial services, insurance and postal services. Businesses below the registration threshold (£90,000 turnover) need not register. Exports outside the UK are zero-rated. Small businesses can apply for the Flat Rate Scheme to simplify VAT accounting.
When do you pay VAT to HMRC?+
Most VAT-registered businesses submit quarterly VAT returns online and pay within one month and seven days of the period end. Monthly returns are available for businesses that regularly reclaim VAT. Annual Accounting Scheme allows annual returns. Late payment attracts surcharges starting at 2% for the first default. HMRC can also charge penalties for errors on returns.
