Frequently Asked Questions
How much is the UK State Pension in 2026?+
The full new State Pension for 2025/26 is £221.20 per week (approximately £11,502 per year). This applies to those reaching State Pension age on or after 6 April 2016 who have 35 qualifying years of National Insurance contributions. Those with fewer years receive a proportionally reduced amount, with a minimum of 10 qualifying years needed to receive anything.
When can I claim my State Pension?+
The current State Pension age is 66 for both men and women. It will rise to 67 between 2026 and 2028, and to 68 between 2044 and 2046 under current legislation. You do not receive the State Pension automatically — you must claim it. HMRC will write to you about 2 months before you reach State Pension age.
How much should I save for retirement?+
A common rule of thumb is to aim for a retirement income of two-thirds of your pre-retirement salary. Financial experts often recommend the Pensions and Lifetime Savings Association's Retirement Living Standards: £14,400/year for a minimum lifestyle, £31,300/year for moderate, and £43,100/year for comfortable (single person, 2024). Start contributing as early as possible to maximise compound growth.
What is auto-enrolment?+
Auto-enrolment requires employers to automatically enrol eligible employees into a workplace pension. The minimum total contribution is 8% of qualifying earnings: at least 3% from the employer and 5% from the employee. You can opt out, but this is generally not advisable as you would lose the employer contribution — essentially free money added to your pension.
